# Executive summary

Sovra gives every person a team of AI helpers that run on computers the
organisation owns, keep their knowledge there, and ask a person before
anything leaves. It is offered through a measured pilot, so the organisation
can see that it pays off.

## The problem

Organisations are being asked to become **AI-native**: to have AI do a large
share of routine work. The UAE, for example, plans to run half of government
work on AI that acts within two years, and Abu Dhabi aims to be the first
fully AI-native government by 2027 ([09](09-sources.md)).

Two things go wrong on the way.

1. **The value does not arrive.** In MIT's 2025 study, 95% of organisations
   studied saw no measurable return from generative AI. The diagnosis was a
   **learning gap**: tools that do not remember and do not fit the work.
   Gartner predicted that at least 30% of projects would be abandoned after
   the proof of concept.
2. **Sovereignty is lost.** Rented AI moves the work, and what the AI learns
   about the organisation, onto someone else's computers, usually in another
   country. Staff use these tools anyway: in the same study, workers at over
   90% of firms used AI while only 40% of firms had bought it.

## Sovra's answer

**Sovereignty you can check.** Seven layers, from data to jurisdiction, each
labelled Today, Next or Direction
([02 — Sovereignty model](02-sovereignty-model.md)). Today:

- memory, files, logs and settings live on the computer;
- keys sit in the operating system's keychain;
- there is no telemetry;
- the AI model is a replaceable guest, and can run on the same computer;
- nothing is sent, run or emailed without a person approving it.

Every claim has a file reference in
[03 — Sovereignty evidence](03-sovereignty-evidence.md).

**Value you can count.** Sovra aims directly at the learning gap:

- memory where every detail quotes its source and corrections keep history;
- files read where they are;
- a team of 12 helpers and 49 ready-made skills that produce finished, saved
  work;
- schedules for routine jobs.

Each capability is mapped to an outcome and a measure in
[04 — Value drivers](04-value-drivers.md).

**A pilot built to end in a decision.**
- Fixed length, a few named people, three real tasks each.
- A baseline measured before day 1.
- Success *and* stop criteria agreed up front.
- Your team holds the numbers.

See [06 — Measuring value](06-measuring-value.md).

## The vision

The same data shape that makes memory correctable today is designed to make
it shareable and auditable later
([05 — Enterprise vision](05-enterprise-vision.md)):

| Stage | Status | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person | **Today** | A private team of helpers on one computer. |
| A group | **Next** | A few computers share helpers and knowledge in encrypted lanes, with no central server. |
| A company | **Direction** | Ledger memory, residency reports, company search, and the organisation's own AI trained on its own computers. |
| Regulated work | **Direction** | Encryption at rest, audit logs, legal hold, an air-gapped edition and certifications. |

## Honest limits

Today Sovra:

- is English-only in its interface;
- bundles no Arabic model;
- leads first-time setup with a cloud key;
- has web search on by default;
- cannot update itself;
- has no certifications, app-level encryption at rest or audit log yet.

A fully local setup is possible with shipped options, but it is not the
default. See [08 — Limits and objections](08-limits-and-objections.md).

## The ask

Choose a sponsor and three tasks. Measure today's starting point. Run a
fixed-length pilot on computers you own. Decide on a date whether to grow,
change or stop.
