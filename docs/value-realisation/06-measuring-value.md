# Measuring value

How a customer and Sovra agree what "better" means, measure it, and decide
whether to grow, change or stop. This is the method behind the pilot offer in
the [value](../decks/value/index.html) and [UAE](../decks/uae/index.html) decks.

## The rule

Sovra never states a return on investment for a customer. The customer
measures a starting point, the customer measures the result, and both sides
agree the test before the pilot starts. Projections, if any, are labelled
projections.

The reason is the evidence in [09 — Sources](09-sources.md): most AI pilots
stall after the proof of concept because nobody agreed what success meant, the
data and risk controls were not ready, or the value was never measured.

## 1. Write a value hypothesis per outcome

One sentence per outcome:

> We expect **[measure]** to move from **[baseline]** to **[target]** by
> **[date]**, because **[capability]** does **[mechanism]**.

Example:

> We expect **minutes to prepare a renewal brief** to move from **the
> measured baseline** to **half of it** by **the end of week six**, because
> **helpers read the contract folder in place and remember the client's
> terms**, so staff stop re-collecting the same context.

Pick two or three hypotheses, not ten. Choose them from the value map in
[04 — Value drivers](04-value-drivers.md).

## 2. Measure the baseline before day 1

- Choose three real, repeated tasks per person.
- Time them the current way, twice, before Sovra is installed.
- Record incidents too: messages sent to the wrong person, files sent outside
  the organisation, transfer reviews raised.
- Write the numbers down where both sides can see them.

No baseline means no claim. If the baseline is skipped, the pilot can still
run, but its result is described in words, not numbers.

## 3. Pair leading and lagging indicators

Leading indicators move first and say whether the pilot is on track. Lagging
indicators are the result the sponsor cares about.

| Leading (weekly) | Where to read it in the app today | Lagging (at decision date) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished work saved per person | **Artifacts** — each saved item and its versions | Time to finish a document |
| Details learned and corrected | **Knowledge** — each detail with its source | Minutes per routine task |
| Jobs handed over | **Schedules** — one-time and repeating jobs | Hours of routine work removed |
| Actions approved, edited, refused | The approval cards and send receipts | Incidents (target: none) |
| Folders connected, links approved | **Knowledge → Files** | Days to first real workflow |
| Share of work on a local model | **Models** — which connections are set up (local or cloud); confirm in the pilot review | Transfer reviews no longer needed |

All of these are read on the person's own computer. Nothing is reported to
Sovra; there is no telemetry (see [03](03-sovereignty-evidence.md)). A pilot
review is therefore a short screen-share or a person reading their own counts
aloud.

## 4. Stage the time to value

| Stage | What should be true | Typical check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | First useful result **saved**, not only answered. | One saved artifact per person. |
| Week 2 | First real workflow: one folder connected, one weekly job scheduled. | Leading indicators moving. |
| Decision date | Lagging measures compared with the baseline. | Sponsor decides. |
| After | Grow what worked: more people today; shared knowledge across a group of computers is **Next** ([05](05-enterprise-vision.md)). | New hypotheses for the next group. |

Time to first value is measured as time to the **first thing saved**, not the
first answer. An answer that is not kept is not yet value.

## 5. Design the pilot

- **Fixed length**, for example six weeks.
- **Few named people**, each with three real tasks.
- **Computers the customer owns**. No Sovra server is involved.
- **Boundaries written down**: which models (local, UAE-hosted or a named
  cloud), which folders, which connections (WhatsApp, Telegram, Google, add-on
  tools) are allowed.
- **Security in the room from day 1**: the data protection and security leads
  see where data lives ([03](03-sovereignty-evidence.md)) and who approves.
- **Success and stop criteria** written and signed before day 1.

Stop criteria are as important as success criteria. Example: "If fewer than
half of participants save work in week 2, we stop and review."

## 6. Mutual success plan (template)

| Item | What it says | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor | The person who decides (e.g. Chief AI Officer, COO). | Customer |
| Outcomes | Two or three value hypotheses. | Customer sponsor |
| Baseline | Measured minutes, days and incidents for the chosen tasks. | Customer team, with Sovra |
| Boundaries | Allowed models, folders and connections. | Customer security / data protection |
| Enablement | Install, first-day session, a contact for questions. | Sovra |
| Cadence | 15-minute weekly look at leading indicators; one mid-point review. | Both |
| Decision | Date, test, and what "grow" means (people, teams, next stage). | Customer sponsor |

## 7. Decision gates

At the decision date there are three honest outcomes:

1. **Grow** — hypotheses met. Write new hypotheses for the next group of
   people.
2. **Change** — some met. Change tasks, model or boundaries; run a short
   second period.
3. **Stop** — not met. Record why. A clean stop is a good outcome; it saves
   the customer money and tells Sovra what to fix.

## What Sovra does not measure for you

- No usage dashboard is sent to Sovra.
- No automatic ROI figure is calculated in the app.
- Counts are read on each computer. A future, opt-in, customer-owned report
  across a group of computers belongs with the group work in stage 2 and is
  not built.
